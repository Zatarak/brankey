Co-Being

Remember learning to ride a bike? First, you needed training wheels and someone holding the back of your seat - complete dependency. Then came the wobbly solo attempts at independence, perhaps with a few scrapes along the way. But the real magic? That was when you could ride alongside others, weaving between friends, adjusting your speed, sometimes leading, sometimes following - that's interdependence in action. CoBeing lives in this dance between dependency, independence, and interdependence. It's about recognizing that while we can stand on our own, we're also part of something larger. Think of a jazz band - each musician is skilled independently, but true magic happens when they play together, responding to each other's rhythms, creating something none could achieve alone. We start life entirely dependent on others - not just for food and shelter, but for love, language and learning. As we grow, we naturally push for independence, like teenagers asserting their autonomy. It's a necessary phase, but it's not the final destination. The real growth comes in realizing that independence doesn't mean isolation. CoBeing emerges when we understand that strength isn't just about standing alone - it's about knowing when to lean in and when to offer support. It's like a forest ecosystem where trees appear to stand independently but actually share nutrients and information through underground networks. They thrive not by competing, but by contributing to the whole. Sometimes our culture celebrates independence so much that we forget the beauty of interconnection. We might feel shame about needing help or fear being seen as weak. But CoBeing challenges this narrative. It suggests that true maturity lies in embracing both our self-sufficiency and our connections to others. Think about your closest relationships - they probably involve a fluid dance of giving and receiving. One day you're the supporter, the next you're supported. This natural rhythm of interdependence doesn't diminish your independence; it enriches it. Like skilled dance partners, each person's strength makes the partnership stronger. CoBeing invites us to see that we're neither fully dependent nor completely independent, but beautifully interdependent. It's about finding that sweet spot where our individual growth nurtures collective flourishing and where community support empowers personal development. In practice, CoBeing means recognizing when to ask for help and when to offer it, when to step forward and when to step back. It's about building relationships and communities where everyone can be both strong and vulnerable, independent and connected. After all, even the mightiest oak needs the right conditions to grow - soil, rain, sun, and yes, other trees.
